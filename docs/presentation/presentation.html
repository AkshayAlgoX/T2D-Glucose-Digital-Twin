From a Reactive Reading to a Predictive Trajectory

The Clinical Problem and Monitoring Hypothesis

02 / 12

The Current Standard: Reactive Point Alarms

  • Traditional Continuous Glucose Monitors (CGMs) alert after glucose breaches static thresholds (e.g., >180 mg/dL).
  • Post-prandial glucose excursions occur rapidly (30–60 min rise time); threshold alarms leave minimal intervention window.
  • Lack of physiological context leads to alert fatigue and high false alarm rates.

Our Approach: Continuous Forward Visibility

  • Forecast future glucose trajectories across a 120-minute horizon in 5-minute discretizations.
  • Quantify predictive uncertainty via calibrated quantile intervals (p05 to p95).
  • Estimate cumulative spike hazard and adverse event probability in real-time.
  • Decompose contributing factors into mechanistic and behavioral drivers.
Clinical Boundary: The clinical hypothesis is that forward visibility aids timely dietary review; measured patient health outcomes or HbA1c reductions have not yet been evaluated in clinical trials.

Two Information Streams, One Patient Context

Observable Static EHR and Dynamic Telemetry Ingestion

03 / 12

Stream 1: Static EHR Patient Context

Captured at baseline / onboarding via validated schema:

  • Demographics: Age, sex, BMI, weight.
  • Metabolic Markers: Baseline HbA1c, eGFR, basal fasting glucose.
  • Clinical History: Diabetes duration, diagnostic status (Healthy, Prediabetes, T2D).
  • Medication Context: Metformin, SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA, DPP-4i, insulin.

Stream 2: Dynamic Telemetry & Wearable Streams

Ingested continuously on a synchronized 5-minute temporal grid:

  • Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) readings (mg/dL).
  • Cardiorespiratory Wearable Signals: Heart Rate (HR), Heart Rate Variability (HRV).
  • Physical Activity & Sleep: Step counts, sleep stage indicators.
  • Logged Dietary Events: Carbohydrate intake timestamps and meal size.

Strict Observable-Input Boundary (Contract S3)

The deployed model strictly consumes observable clinical and wearable features. Privileged simulation latents (unannounced meal sizes, true plasma insulin) are isolated to the simulator environment and prohibited from entering model inputs.

Hybrid Architecture: Physics Prior + Deep Residuals

Unscented Kalman Filter S3 + Temporal Fusion Transformer

04 / 12

1. Mechanistic UKF Prior

Two-compartment Bergman minimal glucose-insulin model with gut absorption dynamics:

  • Tracks unobserved physiological states (insulin sensitivity, carbs-on-board).
  • Provides deterministic physiological rollouts over 120 minutes.

2. PR-TFT Neural Architecture

Physics-Residual Temporal Fusion Transformer:

  • Variable Selection Networks (VSN) for static and time-varying inputs.
  • LSTM encoder/decoder capturing temporal dependencies.
  • Causal multi-head self-attention for long-range interactions.

3. Residual Combination

The neural network learns the residual error over the physics prior:

  • Final forecast = UKF physiological prior + PR-TFT learned quantile residuals.
  • Predicts 7 quantiles (p05, p10, p25, p50, p75, p90, p95).
  • Separate hazard head predicts spike probability.
Architectural Finding: Arm D (PR-TFT + UKF S3) enforces physiological plausibility during data gaps while allowing deep learning to capture complex non-linear habits and wearable interactions.

What the Model Returns: Trajectories & Risk

Standardized ForecastResponse v1 API Schema

05 / 12

Multi-Horizon Quantile Trajectory

  • Temporal Horizon: 120 minutes forward from current as_of timestamp.
  • Discretization: 24 discrete 5-minute timesteps.
  • Probabilistic Quantiles: p05, p10, p25, p50 (median), p75, p90, p95.
  • Conformal Calibration: Saved conformal interval widening fits the empirical 80% (p10–p90) confidence band with isotonic hazard calibration.

Adverse Event Risk Estimation

  • Spike Event Definition: Glucose rising ≥ 50 mg/dL within 60 min and exceeding 180 mg/dL for ≥ 15 min.
  • Cumulative Risk Curve: Onset probability evaluated across 5-min to 120-min offsets.
  • Risk Tiers: Low (<35%), Watch (35–60%), High/Warning (≥60%).
  • Stateful Alert Policy: Hysteresis filtering and 120-min suppression prevent alert flapping.
Verified Contract: All output packets strictly validate against Zod and Pydantic ForecastResponse schemas before reaching client presentation layers.

Adverse Event Warning: Beyond Raw AUROC

Balancing Sensitivity, False Alarm Rates, and Decision Windows

07 / 12

Clinical Evaluation Dimensions

  • Event Sensitivity: Proportion of actual spike episodes detected in advance.
  • False Alerts per Patient-Day (FA/day): Ensuring alert thresholds do not overwhelm clinical workflows.
  • Effective Lead Time: Median advance warning window (minutes before glucose breaches threshold).
  • Decision Actionability: Whether the warning provides sufficient time for dietary adjustment.

Evaluator vs. Serving Policy Disclosures

  • Serving Policy: Employs stateful hysteresis (Watch/Warning) and 120-min suppression for UI display.
  • Scientific Evaluator: Uses independent, stateless point-in-time scoring rules for benchmark comparisons.
  • Warning Lead Time Limit: A 120-min prediction horizon does not imply a 120-min warning lead time. Actual lead times depend on event velocity.
Audit Disclosure: The formal EW-01 scientific evaluation harness remains unexecuted due to incomplete governance sign-offs; no speculative superiority claims over clinical baselines are made.

Explaining the Model Without Claiming Causality

Arithmetic Driver Decomposition in the RiskPanel

08 / 12

Arithmetic Driver Decomposition

The RiskPanel visualizes the relative impact of model features on the predicted glucose trajectory:

  • Learned Correction: Deep residual network delta over the physics prior.
  • Expected Habitual Meal: Population-level meal timing probability.
  • Logged Meals Absorbing: Mechanistic gut absorption from patient logs.
  • Return to Baseline: Physiological clearance and insulin action.

Critical Interpretability Guardrails

  • Internal Decomposition: Bars represent the mathematical contribution of components to the model's net output (mg/dL impact).
  • Not Causal Biology: Feature weights do not establish direct biological causality in a living patient.
  • Unannounced Meal Detection: Disclosed as a heuristic based on UKF innovation residuals, not verified ground truth.
UI Notice: The dashboard includes an explicit disclaimer: “Arithmetic of the model’s own forecast — not a causal attribution.” The contributions add up to the highest point of the forecast median.

Reproducibility & Scientific Limitations

Synthetic Data Realism Gate 3 and Governance Status

Synthetic Data Realism Audit (Gate 3)

Gate 3 Result: FAILED
Discriminator AUROC: 0.9320 (historical, 90% CI: 0.928–0.936) / 0.9290 (duration-corrected, 90% CI: 0.925–0.933) vs. Target: ≤ 0.65.
  • A gradient-boosted discriminator reliably distinguishes synthetic ODE trajectories from mock-real generator records (top tell: jerk_sd, SHAP 0.8878; roc_max_fall).
  • Controlled factorial experiments confirm AR(1) drift (SD 8–16 mg/dL) accounts for partial curvature divergence, but multivariate ODE dynamics remain distinguishable. Null control: AUROC 0.507.
  • Hazard Calibration (Resubstitution Diagnostic): Empirical resubstitution diagnostics on 47,629 at-risk anchors show 14.2% anchor alert rate (sensitivity 63.0%, precision 58.7%) at threshold 0.35, and 5.3% anchor alert rate (sensitivity 29.9%, precision 74.5%) at threshold 0.60. Evaluated thresholds are empirical diagnostics on the validation set, not approved clinical operating points; anchor alert fractions do not represent event-level alerts per day.
